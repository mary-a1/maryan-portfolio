![MAALIN Activewear cover](/maalin-cover.png)

## TL;DR

In 2025 I co-launched MAALIN Activewear with my partner Hana — a physical trainer who owns the brand and stocks inventory. I run all the digital operations: product, UX, content, navigation, pricing, and conversion. The Shopify storefront is my product lab — every change to copy, imagery, navigation, and pricing teaches me something I can take back to my day job in product.

We started it because the modest activewear category kept showing the same gap — pieces forced into a tradeoff between performance and identity.

## Why I built this

I work in product at a bank — but banks are slow, regulated, and consensus-driven. I wanted a parallel environment where I could form a hypothesis, ship a change in an hour, and see the result by the end of the day.

Hana brought the brand vision, the activewear domain expertise, and the inventory. I brought the product and digital operations side — the Shopify storefront, the UX decisions, the funnel work. Shopify was the obvious tool. It also turned out to be the more important lesson — running a real store has taught me more about commerce, conversion, and merchant pain points than any product course ever could.

---

## What I was actually trying to solve

I was not trying to "launch a brand."

**I was trying to understand what actually drives:**
- attention
- interest
- and real intent

**At a high level, I wanted to:**
- Build something users genuinely resonate with
- Align product, brand, and experience
- Identify what drives real demand — not just visibility

**At a working level, I wanted to:**
- Test which content formats create engagement versus action
- Understand where users drop off in the journey
- Validate which products generate actual interest

---

## How the system actually worked

MAALIN did not operate as a traditional funnel. User behaviour formed a continuous loop.

![MAALIN System](/maalin-system.png)

- **Content** created discovery.
- **Engagement** indicated early signal.
- **Exploration** reflected intent.
- **Decision** revealed either conversion or drop off.

**Over time, patterns became clear:**
- High engagement did not always translate into intent
- Visibility alone did not guarantee conversion

The system wasn't predefined — it was shaped through user behaviour and refined through iteration.

---

## What I measured

I treated each stage of the loop as its own measurement problem — different metrics, different insights, different actions. This table is the working artifact I kept updating as the store evolved:

| What I Measured | Key Insight | Action Taken |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery (Content)** — Impressions, Reach, Profile Visits | Lifestyle content outperformed static product shots. Showing the product on a real person increased engagement and interest. | Shifted toward real-life styling, movement, and context. Focused on _how_ the product is worn, not just displayed. |
| **Engagement (Signal)** — Likes, Saves, Shares, Comments | Engagement varied across platforms — TikTok drove higher saves, while Instagram remained lower overall. External exposure (tags, partnerships) drove strongest reach. | Prioritized shareable content over passive engagement. Leaned into partnerships to expand beyond owned channels. |
| **Exploration (Intent)** — Profile Clicks, Product Views, Time on Page | Users consistently showed interest but lacked confidence to proceed. Sizing and fit uncertainty was the biggest hesitation point. | Added size charts and a "how to measure" visual guide. Expanded product descriptions to clarify fit and material. |
| **Conversion (Decision)** — Add to Cart, Checkout, Purchase / Exit | The largest drop-off occurred at checkout — despite clear upstream interest. Hesitation came from lack of confidence, not lack of interest. | Simplified navigation between tops and bottoms. Improved product clarity to support confident decisions. |

---

## What I prioritized (and what I didn't)

#### 🌱 Learning over scale.
Rather than expanding quickly, I focused on understanding what actually drove user behaviour.

#### 💡 Clarity over feature expansion.
Instead of adding more products or options, I focused on reducing uncertainty around fit, material, and use.

#### 🚀 Intent over engagement.
High interaction did not necessarily indicate demand — so decisions were based on progression, not visibility.

---

## What this taught me about commerce

A few things stood out across the whole loop:

**1. High engagement does not mean intent.**
The product pages with the longest time-on-page weren't the highest converters. Some were the lowest — people were _researching_, not _buying_. I stopped using time-on-page as a success metric and started watching scroll-depth past the fit section as a stronger intent signal.

**2. Returning visitors converted at meaningfully higher rates than new visitors.**
This shifted the content strategy — instead of trying to convert first-time visitors, we focused on giving people a reason to come back.

**3. The biggest friction was confidence, not awareness.**
Customers who arrived via Instagram (high-context, lots of pre-existing trust) converted at multiples of the rate of customers from search (low-context). _The product job wasn't getting attention; it was earning trust._

---

## What I'd do differently if I had a team

1. **Run real A/B tests, not sequential changes.** I made changes one at a time and watched the numbers, but I can't fully attribute results without a control. Next time, real A/B tests from day one — even with a free Shopify A/B tool.
2. **Set up proper analytics earlier.** GA4 + Hotjar/Clarity from day one.
3. **Talk to more customers.** Hana and I have customer service messages and order notes that are qualitative data we underused. Next time I'd schedule short calls with the first 20 customers.
4. **Measure decision time, not session length.** A user who decides quickly is a happier user.

---

## What this changed in my approach

**Across the system, engagement signals were strong — but conversion remained the primary point of breakdown.**

I stopped optimizing for engagement — and started focusing on progression.

#### I learned that:
- Attention is easy to generate, but difficult to convert
- Users don't need more options — they need more clarity
- The biggest friction point is often confidence, not awareness

#### This experience reinforced that:
<p style="text-align: center; font-weight:700; ">Product decisions are not just about building features — but about reducing uncertainty at every step of the user journey.</p>

---

## Why this matters

MAALIN isn't a tech company and the stakes are small — but the discipline is the same as my day job in product: form a hypothesis, ship a small change, watch the data, learn, move on. The difference is that here I own the entire digital experience end-to-end — product, UX, content, navigation, conversion — which forces a tighter feedback loop than most product teams ever experience. Hana handles the brand and inventory side; I handle everything the customer sees and clicks.

It's also the project that taught me to trust signals over interest. Attention is easy to generate. Confidence is the harder problem, and that's where the real product work lives.
